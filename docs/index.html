What VICTOR does

VICTOR compares cluster sets: the results of different clustering algorithms, or of one algorithm with different parameters, on the same objects. It scores how similar every pair of results is and shows the scores as tables and plots. It does not run clustering itself and it does not pick a best result. Everything is calculated in your browser; your files are never sent anywhere.

File formats

Cluster set. A text file with one cluster per line and two tab-separated columns: the cluster name, then its members separated by commas. No header.

cluster1	F18,F22,F28,F32
cluster2	F05,F10,F14
cluster3	F06,F11

Network. An edge list with two columns (source, target) or three (plus a numeric weight), separated by tabs or spaces. Duplicate edges are merged with their weights summed and self-loops are dropped.

N1	N10	0.8
N2	N11	0.3

Preparing sets for comparison

The compared sets must contain identical objects. All metrics except the two for overlapping clusterings also need strict partitions, where each object is in exactly one cluster. When the selected sets differ in their objects, use the filters in the Files tab:

  • Cluster size range drops clusters that are too small or too large, for example singletons.
  • Intersection keeps only the objects that every selected set contains.
  • Superset keeps all objects and gives each set a singleton cluster for every object it lacks.

Comparison metrics

All 27 metrics are similarities: higher means more alike, and 1 means identical cluster sets. For a pair shown as "A vs B", the asymmetric metrics treat A as the reference. Below, n is the number of objects, mij the number of objects shared by cluster i of A and cluster j of B, H the entropy and I the mutual information.

Counting pairs

Adjusted Rand Index
Agreement on object pairs, corrected for chance. Around 0 for unrelated sets; can be negative.
Rand Index
Fraction of object pairs on which the two sets agree. Drifts towards 1 when there are many clusters, so prefer the adjusted version.
Fowlkes-Mallows Index
Geometric mean of the two Wallace criteria.
Jaccard Index
Pairs co-clustered in both sets, divided by pairs co-clustered in at least one.
Overlap Coefficient
Pairs co-clustered in both sets, divided by the smaller of the two sets' co-clustered pair counts.
Wallace Criterion 1 and 2
Probability that a pair co-clustered in A is also co-clustered in B (type 1), and the reverse (type 2). Asymmetric.

Set overlaps and matching

Maximum-Match Measure
Clusters are paired greedily by largest overlap; the matched overlaps are summed and divided by n.
F-Measure
Each cluster of A takes its best F-score, 2mij / (|Ai| + |Bj|), over the clusters of B; the scores are averaged weighted by cluster size. Asymmetric.
Optimal-Match Accuracy
Like the Maximum-Match Measure, but the one-to-one pairing of clusters is the best possible one (Hungarian algorithm) instead of a greedy one, so it is never lower. It equals 1 minus the classification error distance.
Purity (Meila-Heckerman)
Each cluster of A takes its largest overlap with a cluster of B; the overlaps are summed and divided by n. Asymmetric.
Van Dongen Measure
The Van Dongen distance, 2n minus the row maxima and the column maxima of the confusion matrix, divided by 2n and subtracted from 1.
B-Cubed Precision, Recall, F-Score
Per-object scores averaged over all objects. Precision: the share of an object's B cluster that is also in its A cluster. Recall: the share of its A cluster that is also in its B cluster. The F-Score is their harmonic mean. Precision and recall are asymmetric.
Element-Centric Similarity
Per-object score averaged over all objects (Gates et al. 2019). For strict partitions it is the number of objects sharing both of the object's clusters, divided by the size of the larger of the two. It is symmetric and never exceeds either B-Cubed precision or recall.

Mutual information

NMI by Strehl & Ghosh
I divided by the geometric mean of the two entropies.
NMI by Fred & Jain
I divided by the arithmetic mean of the two entropies. This is also the V-Measure.
NMI by the larger entropy
I divided by the larger of the two entropies; the strictest normalization.
Adjusted Mutual Information
I corrected for chance: (I − E[I]) / (mean entropy − E[I]), where E[I] is the value expected for random sets with the same cluster sizes. Prefer it to NMI when the compared sets have very different numbers of clusters.
Normalized Variation of Information
½(H(A|B)/H(A) + H(B|A)/H(B)), subtracted from 1.
Variation of Information
H(A) + H(B) − 2I, divided by its upper bound log n and subtracted from 1.
Variation of Information by joint entropy
The same distance divided by the joint entropy H(A,B) and subtracted from 1. Unlike the log n version it reaches 0 for independent sets of any size.
Homogeneity and Completeness
Homogeneity, I / H(A), is 1 when every cluster of B lies inside one cluster of A. Completeness, I / H(B), is 1 when every cluster of A lies inside one cluster of B. Together they show whether one set refines the other. Asymmetric.

Overlapping clusterings

These two are the only metrics that accept sets in which an object belongs to several clusters. When a selected set overlaps, only they are calculated. They also work on strict partitions.

Omega Index
The Adjusted Rand Index generalized to overlaps (Collins & Dent 1988): two sets agree on a pair of objects when the pair shares the same number of clusters in both; the agreement is corrected for chance. On strict partitions it equals the Adjusted Rand Index.
Overlapping NMI
The measure of Lancichinetti, Fortunato & Kertész (2009). Every cluster is treated as a yes/no property of the objects and is explained by its best-matching cluster in the other set; the remaining uncertainty is averaged over both directions and subtracted from 1. On strict partitions it is not equal to the ordinary NMI.

Plots

  • Bar plot: one bar per pair of cluster sets, sorted by similarity.
  • Heatmap: all pairs in a grid, with rows and columns ordered by average-linkage hierarchical clustering so that similar sets sit together. The diagonal is left neutral.
  • Sankey: cluster sets as blocks joined by bands. Band thickness is relative to the other pairs shown, not an absolute scale.
  • Network: cluster sets as nodes; thicker edges mean more similar sets. Drag nodes to rearrange.
  • Circos: cluster sets as arcs; ribbons join the pairs, shaded by similarity.
  • UpSet (Files tab): appears when the selected sets do not contain the same objects. Each column is a combination of sets, marked by the filled dots; its bar counts the objects found in exactly those sets and no others. The column with every dot filled is what the Intersection filter keeps.

"Hide below" removes the pairs whose similarity is under the chosen value. Every plot has an Export button that saves it as PNG, SVG, PDF, JPEG or WebP at up to six times the on-screen resolution, on a theme-coloured, white or transparent background.

Network quality

Load a network and choose a cluster set to see how well its clusters are separated on that network.

Conductance
Weight of the edges leaving the cluster, divided by the smaller of the cluster's volume and the rest of the network's volume (volume is the sum of weighted degrees). Closer to 0 is better.
Internal density
Edges inside the cluster divided by the number of possible edges inside it.
Cut ratio
Edges leaving the cluster divided by the number of possible edges between the cluster and the rest.
Modularity
How much more edge weight falls inside clusters than expected at random, for the whole cluster set. Network nodes outside every cluster count as singletons.
Coverage
Share of the total edge weight that lies inside clusters.
