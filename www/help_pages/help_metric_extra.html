Additional cluster set comparison metrics
Besides the ten metrics described in the Metrics tab, VICTOR offers twelve more. All of them are similarities: higher values mean more similar cluster sets. In what follows, C and C' are two cluster sets of the same n objects, M is their confusion matrix with elements mij, H is the entropy and I the Mutual Information. For the asymmetric metrics, the first cluster set of a pair ("A" in "A vs B") is C.

Counting Pairs

Rand Index (rand). The fraction of object pairs on which the two cluster sets agree (co-clustered in both or separated in both). Ranges in [0, 1]. It approaches 1 when the number of clusters is large compared to the number of objects, so the Adjusted Rand Index is usually preferable.

Set Overlaps / Matching

F-Measure (f). Each cluster of C is matched to the cluster of C' with which it has the highest F-score (harmonic mean of precision and recall), and these scores are averaged, weighted by cluster size. Asymmetric. Calculated by mclustcomp.

Meila-Heckerman Measure (mhm). Each cluster of one set is matched to its best-overlapping cluster of the other set and the overlaps are summed and divided by n. Asymmetric. Calculated by mclustcomp.

Van Dongen Measure, normalized and inverted (vdm_norm). The Van Dongen distance is 2n − Σi maxj mij − Σj maxi mij. VICTOR divides it by 2n and subtracts the result from 1, so the value is in (0, 1] and 1 describes identical cluster sets.

B-Cubed Precision, Recall and F-Score (bcubed_p, bcubed_r, bcubed_f). Element-level scores. For each object, precision is the fraction of the objects in its C' cluster that also share its C cluster, and recall is the fraction of the objects in its C cluster that also share its C' cluster. Both are averaged over all objects: precision = (1/n) Σij mij2 / |C'j| and recall = (1/n) Σij mij2 / |Ci|. The F-Score is their harmonic mean and is symmetric. All range in (0, 1].

Mutual Information

Normalized Mutual Information by Danon (nmi3). A third normalization of the Mutual Information, calculated by mclustcomp.

Adjusted Mutual Information (ami). The Mutual Information corrected for chance: AMI = (I − E[I]) / (½(H(C) + H(C')) − E[I]), where E[I] is the Mutual Information expected between random cluster sets with the same cluster sizes. It is 1 for identical cluster sets and around 0 for unrelated ones (it can be slightly negative). Prefer it to the Normalized Mutual Information when the compared cluster sets have very different numbers of clusters.

Variation of Information, normalized by log n and inverted (vi_norm). VI = H(C) + H(C') − 2I, divided by its upper bound log n and subtracted from 1. It differs from the Normalized Variation of Information of the Metrics tab only in the normalization.

Homogeneity and Completeness (homogeneity, completeness). Homogeneity = I / H(C) is 1 when every cluster of C' lies inside a single cluster of C. Completeness = I / H(C') is 1 when every cluster of C lies inside a single cluster of C'. Together they show whether one cluster set is a refinement of the other. Their harmonic mean (the V-Measure) equals the Normalized Mutual Information by Fred and Jain, which is why it is not listed separately.

Cluster quality on a network (Conductance tab)
After executing the Conductance tab, the Cluster Quality sub tab lists for every cluster: the number of its objects found in the network, the number of internal edges and cut edges, its conductance (cut weight divided by the smaller of the volumes of the cluster and of the rest of the network), its internal density (internal edges divided by the number of possible internal edges) and its cut ratio (cut edges divided by the number of possible edges between the cluster and the rest of the network). Above the table, the modularity of the cluster set on the network and its coverage (fraction of the edge weight that lies inside clusters) are reported. Duplicate edges are merged (weights summed) and self-loops are removed before the calculation.
